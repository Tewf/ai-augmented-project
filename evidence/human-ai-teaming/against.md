# Against

The evidence against what the front page assumes, and the disagreements. Sources are in [review](review.md). None is deleted to make the setup look stronger.

## Against "the model helps"

- Experienced developers were 19% slower with AI tools in a trial of 16 people (Becker 2025). They believed they were 20% faster.
- Human-AI teams scored below the better of person and model alone on average (Vaccaro 2024).
- Explanations of a model's answers raised acceptance of wrong answers (Bansal 2021).

## Against "write a context file"

- Generated files did not raise success and cost over 20% more on average (Gloaguen 2026).
- Repository overviews, the most recommended content, did not help (Gloaguen 2026).
- A second ablation found no effect over 288 runs (Khatri 2026, secondary).
- A stale file is obeyed over the code, even when the agent notices the conflict (Xu 2026, secondary).

## Disagreements

| Point | One side | Other side |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | 55.8% faster (Peng 2023, secondary); about 21% faster (Paradis 2024, secondary) | 19% slower (Becker 2025) |
| Context files | developer files +2.4% (Gloaguen 2026) | no effect (Khatri 2026) |
| Length | under 200 lines (Anthropic 2026b); 32 KiB (OpenAI 2026b) | no study sets a length |

- A possible reason for the speed split: task, people and tool differ. Paradis 2024 warns its effect may not carry across tools (abstract, secondary).
- A possible reason for the context split: task difficulty is agent-specific (Khatri 2026, secondary).

## Against the sources themselves

- Most context-file studies are 2025 and 2026 preprints, not yet peer reviewed.
- The trials are small or on one company's tools.
- The vendor pages describe their owners' products and the owners' own practice.
